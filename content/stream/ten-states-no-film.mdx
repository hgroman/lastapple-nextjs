In June we drove across the country and flew a drone most mornings. The catalogue says what came back: 346 clips, 203 of them video, 122.1 gigabytes, ten states. Every flight logged with its own coordinates as it was shot.

Then a map got generated from that telemetry, deployed, and left alone.

<SkiesMap slug="cross-country-2026" />

## The part worth admitting

That map went live on 26 June, the day the trip ended. It is, as of this writing, two months old.

**Nothing on the internet has ever linked to it.** Not the site, not a post, not an email. It sat at a machine-generated address with nobody's name on it, doing nothing, while we told people we had been out shooting. The work was finished and the distribution was never started — which is a different failure from not doing the work, and an easier one to miss, because everything on your own screen looks done.

We only found it because someone went looking for what already existed instead of proposing to build it again.

## What the map actually knows

Each dot is a real flight with real coordinates. They are coloured by the light they were shot in, and that is measured rather than asserted — the catalogue stores sun elevation per clip, so "sunrise" means the sun was four degrees above the horizon, not that the footage looked warm. Fifty-two of the clips fall in golden hour by that definition.

Two of the 346 carry no GPS at all. That is why the map header says 344 and this post says 346: one number counts what can be plotted, the other counts what exists. Neither is wrong and neither is "the" number, which is the sort of thing that quietly turns into a lie on a marketing page if nobody writes down what they counted.

## And still: no film

Not one finished piece has been scored from this trip yet. Three exist from earlier trips — they are over on [Live-Scored Skies](/skies), each one shown beside the flight path it was cut from — and this route has produced none.

There is no good excuse. The footage is catalogued, the light is classified, the map is built. What is missing is the hours at a piano, and those do not come from a pipeline.

So this is the honest state: ten states of footage, fully mapped, waiting on the slowest part of the process, which is a person deciding what it should sound like. The map goes up now because it is real now. The films will follow, and when they do they will appear next to this same map, which is the whole point of logging the flights in the first place.
