## What This Is

Most aerial video is footage with a song laid over it. The song was written by someone else, for nobody in particular, and licensed by the minute.

This is the other thing: **the film and the music are made together, by the same person, about the same place.** Hank flies the site, cuts the footage, and then writes the score to it — piano or strings, composed against what is actually on screen. The music exists because the place did.

You can watch three of them on [Live-Scored Skies](/skies). Each one is shown beside the flight path it was cut from.

## What You Get

A finished 4K film with an original score. Alongside it, the thing almost nobody else hands over: **the flight data.**

Every flight is logged with its own coordinates as it is shot, so you receive an interactive map of exactly where the camera was — not an approximation drawn afterwards. Time of day and sun elevation are recorded per clip, which is why "shot at golden hour" is a measurement here rather than a description.

That map is yours. It is also the reason a film on this site can sit next to the route that produced it.

## Why the Scoring Matters

A library track is chosen. A score is written.

The difference shows up in the cut: when the music is composed to the footage, the piece can breathe where the landscape does. A ridge line and a phrase can arrive together, because both were decided by the same person at the same time. That is not available at any price from a stock library, because the library does not know what your site looks like.

If you have ever watched a beautiful aerial video and felt that the music was riding on top of it rather than belonging to it — that is what this is for.

## Who This Isn't For

Straight answers, so nobody wastes a call:

- **High-volume real estate listings.** If you need twelve properties shot this month at a per-listing rate, this is the wrong service and an ordinary drone operator will serve you better and cheaper.
- **Anyone who needs it next week.** The flying is the fast part. Composing and cutting is not, and rushing it produces exactly the generic result you were trying to avoid.
- **Projects that need a crew.** This is one person with a drone and a piano. Closed sets, permits, talent and lighting are a different kind of production.
- **Anyone who wants a library track.** Cheaper, faster, and genuinely fine for many jobs. Just not this one.

## How It Works

1. **A conversation.** What the place is, what it is for, and whether the result should feel still or moving. This is also where we work out whether the location is flyable.
2. **The flights.** Scheduled around light, not convenience — sunrise and the last hour before sunset do most of the work.
3. **The cut.** Footage selected and assembled first, so the music is written to a real edit rather than a guess.
4. **The score.** Composed to the cut, performed and mixed.
5. **Delivery.** The finished film in 4K, plus your flight map.
