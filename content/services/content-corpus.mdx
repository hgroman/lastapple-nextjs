## What This Is

Two problems that turn out to be one.

The first: your work is trapped in a platform that will not hand it back cleanly. There is no bulk export, or there is one and it silently destroys the structure on its way out.

The second, and the expensive one: **nobody actually knows what you have.** Not the count, not which version is the real one, not which of the things that look like duplicates are separate finished pieces.

We get it out, we establish what is canonical, and we make it answerable.

## The Engagement That Defined It

A single working session, seven hours, on a body of work built over years:

- **54 of 54 projects extracted** from a platform offering no bulk export
- **34 distinct manuscripts, 2,296,974 words**, made searchable
- **Chapter structure recovered for all 54** — the platform's own export flattens paragraph styling and loses it, so structure was taken from the editor outline instead

Then the findings, which the author did not have and had not asked for: six unresolved draft clusters, a probable unidentified series, a format anomaly, and resolved provenance on an earlier deliverable.

## The Part That Should Decide Whether You Hire Us

**We proved the "duplicates" were separate finished books before touching anything.**

Fifteen pairs looked like near-identical drafts of the same work. The efficient move — the move a deduplication script makes by default — is to collapse them and keep the longer one. We sampled sixty sentences from each side of each pair instead.

**Fourteen of the fifteen pairs share zero text. Two of them reach opposite endings.**

They were not drafts. They were different novels that happened to be similar in shape and length. **Assuming would have deleted books.**

That is why the deduplication here is deliberately conservative: exact content-hash for true duplicates, similarity scoring to *flag* near matches, and a standing rule not to collapse anything at high similarity without a human looking. A tool tuned to be tidy will destroy work. A tool tuned to be careful gives you a list to review.

## Canonicity Is the Client's Call, Not Ours

Deciding which version is the real one is not a technical question, and we stopped pretending it was.

The author settled it himself, through an accessible survey — 27 works in play, 7 set aside. **He overruled our recommendation on exactly the two questions where we had written "we are guessing."**

That is the design working. We marked our own uncertainty, put it in front of the person who actually knew, and were corrected precisely where we had said we might be. **Honesty about confidence is what made the correction possible** — a confident recommendation on those two would have been accepted and would have been wrong.

We also corrected a figure that had understated the client's own productivity **by a factor of sixteen**, because it had been counting records in our database instead of files in his.

## The Real Cost of Not Knowing

That team spent **March to June** trying to establish what they already had.

In one review, a status table was read off a screen share, check marks were mistaken for dashes, and the conclusion was that a work had no abstract — when it had three layers of one.

**Four months of a team's attention went into a question a generated catalogue answers on load.** That is the actual product: not the dashboard, but the end of the meeting where everyone tries to remember what exists.

The catalogue is generated from source, so it cannot drift; and it is built screen-reader-first, because the person who most needs to read a status table is often not reading it visually.

## Who This Isn't For

**Not for you if your library is small enough to hold in your head.** Under a few dozen assets, a spreadsheet is genuinely better and we will tell you so.

**Not for you if nobody can rule on canonicity.** We can extract, structure and present. Deciding which version is the real one requires someone with the authority and the memory to say, and if that person is not available the project stalls at the interesting part.

**Not for you if you want the duplicates cleaned up automatically.** We will not do that, for the reason above. You will get a list to review, and reviewing it is work.
