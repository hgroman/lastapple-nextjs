## What This Is

You did not set out to run six disconnected tools. It happened one purchase at a time — a CRM here, a scheduler there, an email platform, a store, a chat widget, an accounting package. Each one was the right call on the day.

Now leads fall between the systems, automations break quietly and nobody notices for months, and **no one in the building can describe, end to end, how a new customer actually flows through the business.**

That last one is the real problem. Everything else is a symptom of it.

## The Integration Map

A CRM only earns its keep when everything feeds it. On that account, eight systems converge:

```
   Scheduling      ─┐
   Mail parsing    ─┤
   Live chat       ─┤
   Legacy CRM      ─┼──►   CRM   ──►  Nurture · Routing · Reporting
   Forms           ─┤    (the brain)
   E-commerce      ─┤
   Accounting      ─┤
   Payments        ─┘
```

A demo booked in the scheduler becomes a contact on the right list. A parsed order email creates the record and triggers the follow-up. The accounting system stops being a place where revenue information goes to be invisible.

**This is the part most "CRM help" skips**, because it is unglamorous and it requires understanding a business rather than a product.

## The Half Nobody Does: Retiring What Is Already Broken

Every mature stack accumulates automations that no longer run, run twice, or run against a system that was replaced two years ago. They are invisible from any dashboard, because a workflow that fires into nothing reports success.

On that engagement we traced the order-to-accounting flow end to end and **retired dead paths without breaking the live ones** — which is the entire difficulty. Deleting automations is easy. Knowing which of them something still depends on is the job.

A rescue of the email side ran alongside it: a **376,413-address database scrubbed to 306,259, with dead weight suppressed down to roughly an 83,500-address clean mailable core**, and sender reputation restored after platform suspensions.

**Cutting a list by three quarters is not a loss.** Those addresses were not reachable; they were only being counted.

## Who This Isn't For

**Not for you if the tools are the problem you want solved.** Swapping platforms rarely fixes a process nobody has mapped, and we will usually recommend keeping what you have.

**Not for you if nobody can say what should happen.** We can connect anything to anything. Deciding what *ought* to happen when a lead arrives is a business decision, and it stays yours.

**Not for you if it has to be finished and then forgotten.** Integrations rot. A stack nobody maintains is a stack that will quietly go wrong, and this is a standing arrangement rather than a project.
