## The Three Tiers

### WordPress Essentials — $97/month

For sites that work, don't get touched often, and just need someone watching.

- Core, plugin, and theme updates
- Daily off-site backups (30-day retention)
- Security monitoring and weekly scans
- Uptime monitoring with email alerts
- 1 hour of content updates per month
- Monthly health summary

**Best for:** A 5–15 page brochure site for a service business. The site rarely changes, but you need someone making sure it's patched, backed up, and not hacked.

### Growth Accelerator — $297/month

For sites that are part of how the business actually runs.

Everything in Essentials, plus:
- Performance optimization (quarterly Core Web Vitals pass)
- Malware scanning with active remediation
- Priority response for critical issues (under 4 business hours)
- 3 hours of content updates per month
- Quarterly SEO check-in (Search Console review, schema validation)
- Detailed monthly report with metrics

**Best for:** A site that drives leads, takes form submissions, runs a small WooCommerce catalog, or has an active blog. You need maintenance, but you also need someone paying attention to performance and SEO.

### Business Transformer — $697/month

For sites that are central to the business and evolve constantly.

Everything in Growth Accelerator, plus:
- Advanced SEO implementation (ongoing schema, content optimization, internal linking)
- 8 hours of content or development work per month
- Custom WordPress development (form integrations, CRM hooks, conditional logic)
- Basic CRM integration support (HubSpot, FluentCRM, similar)
- Weekly maintenance check-ins
- Sub-2-hour response on critical issues during business hours

**Best for:** A site that's the front door of a 10+ person business, an active e-commerce shop, or any property where downtime costs real money per hour.

## What Counts as a "Content Update"

To avoid the awkward conversation later: a content update is any change to existing pages or posts that takes under 30 minutes. Swapping a hero image. Adding a new staff bio. Updating pricing on a service page. Editing copy on the homepage.

What's *not* a content update:
- Net-new page builds (those are scoped separately)
- Custom development (forms, integrations, conditional logic)
- Migrations or redesigns (see [Renaissance](/services/website-renaissance))
- Hour-by-hour rush work for a launch

Unused hours don't roll over. You're paying for the standing capacity, not for stockpiled time.

## Before You Pick a Plan — Is Your Site Even Ready?

Some sites aren't ready for ongoing maintenance. They're broken, abandoned, running PHP 7.4, or hacked. Putting a maintenance plan on top of that is throwing good money after bad.

If you're not sure, the [Resurrection tier](/services/wordpress-maintenance#resurrection) handles the one-time work needed to get a site to a state where ongoing maintenance is meaningful. The audit is $500 and is credited toward the engagement.
