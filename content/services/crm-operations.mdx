## What This Is

Most companies own a good CRM and use a fraction of it. The licence is paid, the contacts are in it, and the actual work still happens in someone's inbox and a spreadsheet.

We build the CRM into the place the revenue operation actually runs — architected around how you sell, fed by every system that touches a customer, and maintained afterwards.

## What "Built Properly" Measures Out To

Live figures from one client CRM we operate, verified from the platform's own API on **2026-06-30**. One account, dated, re-derivable:

| | |
|---|---:|
| Workflows built and maintained | **115** (90 live) |
| Audience segments | **618** |
| Sales pipelines architected | **9** |
| Custom fields configured | **433** |
| Lead-capture forms | **55** |
| Under management | **16,213 contacts / 7,863 deals** |
| External systems feeding it | **8**, via ~**19** live automations |

**433 custom fields is not a boast, it is a description of what modelling a real business costs.** The vendor's default object does not know what your business sells or how a deal actually progresses. Nine pipelines exist because there are nine genuinely different ways business is won on that account, and forcing them into one would have made the reporting useless.

## What We Actually Do In It

**CRM architecture and pipeline design.** Model the system around how business is won, then build the fields that make each stage mean something.

**Audience engineering.** Segments built from behaviour and firmographics rather than from whatever fields happened to be filled in.

**Automation and nurture.** Triggered journeys from first enquiry through to renewal — the follow-up that happens whether or not anyone remembers to do it.

**Lead routing and hygiene.** Inbound assigned to the right owner immediately, enriched, de-duplicated. Duplicate records are not an aesthetic problem; they are the reason the numbers do not add up.

**Email and SMS off the same data**, with consent and timing handled once rather than per channel.

**Integration.** Covered on the [systems integration page](/services/systems-integration) — it is the part most CRM help skips.

**Audit and cleanup.** Every mature CRM accumulates automations that fire into nothing, fire twice, or fire at a system that was replaced. They report success. Finding them means tracing what still depends on what, and that is the work — deleting is trivial and knowing what is safe to delete is not.

## Who This Isn't For

**Not for you if you want a migration and then silence.** The value compounds through maintenance. A CRM nobody tends drifts back to a paid address book in about a year.

**Not for you if sales will not change how they work.** We can build the pipeline that reflects reality. If the team then keeps working out of an inbox, the CRM becomes a very expensive record of what was ignored.

**Not for you if the answer is "just clean the data."** We will do that, and it will come back, because dirty data is a symptom of routing and capture rules that were never set.
