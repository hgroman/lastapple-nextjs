## What This Is

Most AI consulting sells you a pilot. This is the other thing: **a governed multi-agent operation running against your real systems, built so it cannot quietly do the wrong thing.**

The distinction matters more than it sounds. An agent that can read your database is a demo. An agent that can *write* to your database, deploy your code, or send mail as your company is an operation — and an operation needs governance that survives the day everybody stops paying attention.

We know where that breaks, because we published ours breaking.

## The Near-Miss We Published

In April, our own AI assistant offered to copy god-mode database credentials into a client project. Not maliciously — helpfully. It looked at how the working project was configured, found the credential launcher, and proposed copying it across.

The credential was full superuser access to a database holding every client's data. We had a written rule against exactly this. It was in the agent's instruction file. The document is titled with the word MANDATORY in capital letters.

Nothing was deployed; we caught it in the proposal. Then we spent five months measuring whether it was a fluke. It wasn't — and the full field report, including the two detection methods we threw away and the metric that lied to us about our own fix, is published at [Why AI Agents Ignore Your Instructions](/stream/why-ai-agents-ignore-instructions).

**That post is the reason to hire us, and it is also the reason to trust the number below.**

## What We Actually Fixed, and How

Across 368 measured sessions, sessions that performed a privileged write before reading a single governing document fell from **23.2% to 4.9%**. Sessions where a human had to stop the agent and say *I already told you this* fell from **9.0% to 2.8%**.

Nothing about the *wording* of the rules changed. We had already spent months escalating emphasis — bolding, then capitals, then MANDATORY — and the number sat still.

What moved it was removing the agent's opportunity to skip the step:

> **An instruction the agent can choose to skip has a compliance rate. An instruction built into the initial conditions doesn't need one.**

That is the whole method, and it is what we build into your system. A rule you have rewritten four times is not a wording problem — it is a design problem, and it has a structural fix: a hook that injects the context, a schema constraint that rejects the write, a wrapper that refuses the call, a file that must exist before the next step runs.

## What You Get

**An agent per function, not one assistant with root.** Each one has a written identity, a scoped credential, and a library of procedures it is expected to follow. Least privilege is the default, not the hardening pass.

**Governance in the initial conditions.** Context injected before the model's first turn, so an agent cannot begin work not knowing its own rules. Constraints at the database layer, so the write fails at insert time rather than relying on diligence. Guards at commit time, so the dangerous change is refused rather than reviewed.

**Measurement you can audit.** Detectors that report whether your agents follow instructions, blind-sampled before anyone trusts them. We will show you the ones that failed our own sampling — one of ours flagged 41% of sessions and was wrong every single time.

**Documentation written for your team, not for us.** The procedure library is plain text in your repository. You can read it, edit it, and keep running without us. If an engagement ends and your system becomes unmaintainable, we built it wrong.

## Who This Isn't For

Straight answers, so nobody wastes a call:

**Not for you if you want a chatbot.** That is a smaller, cheaper, genuinely different project. We build [chatbot solutions](/solutions/ai-chatbot-solutions) too, and if that is the real need we will tell you on the call rather than upsell you into this.

**Not for you if nothing is at stake.** If your agents only read public data and draft copy, this is over-engineered. Governance is worth paying for when an agent can touch money, customer records, production systems, or your outbound reputation.

**Not for you if you want the pilot and not the operation.** We are not the right team for a proof of concept that will be shelved. The work here is the unglamorous half — the credentials, the constraints, the measurement.

**Not for you if "we'll write a stricter prompt" is the preferred answer.** We measured that approach for five months. It has a ceiling, and the ceiling is somewhere below MANDATORY in capital letters.
